Three Branches of Government

Legislative Branch
Makes the laws
The legislative branch is responsible for creating, debating, and passing laws. In the United States, this branch consists of Congress, which is divided into two chambers: the House of Representatives and the Senate.
  • Main Body: U.S. Congress
  • Chambers: House of Representatives (435 members) & Senate (100 members)
  • Key Powers: Make laws, declare war, regulate commerce
  • Term Length: House: 2 years, Senate: 6 years
  • Leadership: Speaker of the House, Senate Majority Leader
Executive Branch
Enforces the laws
The executive branch is responsible for implementing and enforcing laws. It is headed by the President, who serves as both the head of state and the head of government.
  • Head: President of the United States
  • Key Members: Vice President, Cabinet, Executive Departments
  • Key Powers: Veto laws, command military, make treaties
  • Term Length: 4 years (maximum 2 terms)
  • Election: Electoral College
Judicial Branch
Interprets the laws
The judicial branch is responsible for interpreting laws and ensuring they are applied fairly. It consists of the federal court system, with the Supreme Court at its apex.
  • Highest Court: U.S. Supreme Court
  • Key Members: 9 Supreme Court Justices (1 Chief, 8 Associate)
  • Key Powers: Judicial review, interpret Constitution, resolve disputes
  • Term Length: Lifetime appointment
  • Nomination: President nominates, Senate confirms
Bill of Rights
Protects individual freedoms
The Bill of Rights comprises the first ten amendments to the U.S. Constitution. It guarantees essential rights and liberties to individual citizens and limits the power of the government.
  • 1st Amendment: Freedom of speech, religion, press, assembly
  • 2nd Amendment: Right to bear arms
  • 4th Amendment: Protection from unreasonable searches
  • 5th Amendment: Right to due process, protection from self-incrimination
  • 10th Amendment: Powers not given to federal government are reserved for states
